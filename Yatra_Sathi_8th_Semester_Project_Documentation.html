ABSTRACT

Nepal's trekking industry has experienced remarkable growth in recent years, attracting adventurers from around the world to explore the majestic Himalayas. However, solo trekkers often face significant challenges in finding compatible trekking partners who share similar interests, experience levels, and schedules. The absence of dedicated platforms for connecting trekking enthusiasts creates safety concerns, limits social opportunities, and diminishes the overall trekking experience.

This project presents Yatra Sathi, a comprehensive web-based platform designed to address these challenges by facilitating meaningful connections between trekking partners in Nepal. The system employs an advanced multi-factor matching algorithm that evaluates compatibility across six key dimensions: shared interests (30%), experience level (25%), availability timing (15%), geographic location (10%), language preferences (10%), and age compatibility (10%). This sophisticated approach ensures that users are matched with partners who align with their trekking preferences, fitness capabilities, and travel expectations.

The platform is built using modern web technologies including React 18, TypeScript, and Vite for the frontend, with Supabase providing robust backend services including PostgreSQL database, real-time messaging, and authentication. Key features include comprehensive user profile management, AI-powered compatibility scoring with detailed explanations, real-time messaging with read receipts, and an intuitive interface following Material Design principles with a Himalayan-inspired theme.

Implementation follows an incremental development methodology, ensuring systematic feature delivery and continuous quality improvement. The system incorporates Row Level Security policies for data protection, extensive testing including unit and system-level test cases, and performance optimizations using TanStack React Query for efficient state management. Additionally, the platform promotes responsible trekking practices, enhances mountain safety through verified partner connections, and contributes to Nepal's tourism sector by encouraging sustainable outdoor recreation.

Testing results demonstrate the system's reliability across critical functionalities including user authentication, profile management, matching algorithm accuracy, and real-time communication. The platform successfully addresses the identified challenges while providing an engaging user experience that encourages community building among trekking enthusiasts.

Keywords: Trekking Partner Matching, Compatibility Algorithm, Real-time Messaging, Nepal Tourism, Web Application, Supabase, React, User Matching System, Adventure Travel Platform

CHAPTER 1
INTRODUCTION

1.1 Introduction

Nepal, home to eight of the world's fourteen highest peaks including Mount Everest, has established itself as a premier destination for trekking and mountaineering enthusiasts. The country's diverse landscape ranges from the subtropical plains of the Terai to the alpine heights of the Himalayas, offering trekking routes that cater to adventurers of all experience levels. According to the Nepal Tourism Board, trekking tourism contributes significantly to the national economy, with hundreds of thousands of visitors embarking on mountain adventures annually.

Despite this flourishing industry, solo trekkers and adventure seekers face considerable challenges when planning their journeys. Finding compatible trekking partners who share similar fitness levels, experience, interests, and schedules remains a persistent problem. Traditional methods of partner finding—through travel forums, social media groups, or guesthouse bulletin boards—are often inefficient, unreliable, and fail to assess genuine compatibility. This gap in the market creates safety concerns, as trekking alone in remote mountain regions carries inherent risks, and diminishes the social and cultural aspects that make trekking experiences truly memorable.

The importance of finding suitable trekking companions extends beyond mere convenience. Compatible partners enhance safety through mutual support during challenging terrain, share knowledge and experiences that enrich the journey, distribute costs for guides and permits, and create lasting connections within the outdoor adventure community. Furthermore, responsible trekking practices are strengthened when experienced trekkers can mentor beginners, ensuring environmental conservation and cultural sensitivity in Nepal's fragile mountain ecosystems.

Yatra Sathi, which translates to "Travel Companion" in Nepali, addresses these challenges through a sophisticated web-based platform specifically designed for the trekking community. The system employs advanced matching algorithms that evaluate compatibility across multiple dimensions including shared interests, experience levels, timing preferences, geographic proximity, language capabilities, and age demographics. By leveraging modern web technologies and real-time communication features, Yatra Sathi creates a trusted ecosystem where trekkers can discover, connect, and coordinate with like-minded adventure partners.

This platform represents a significant technological contribution to Nepal's tourism sector, promoting both safety and community building among domestic and international trekkers. By facilitating verified connections and providing transparent compatibility assessments, Yatra Sathi encourages responsible mountain tourism while supporting the government's initiatives to diversify and strengthen Nepal's tourism offerings beyond traditional package tours.

1.2 Problem Statement

The current landscape of trekking partner discovery in Nepal presents several critical challenges that Yatra Sathi aims to address:

  1. Safety Concerns in Solo Trekking: Trekking alone in remote mountain regions poses significant risks including altitude sickness, injuries, navigation errors, and emergency situations where immediate assistance is unavailable. The absence of a reliable system for finding trustworthy companions forces many adventurers to trek solo or abandon their plans entirely.
  2. Lack of Compatibility Assessment: Existing informal methods for finding trekking partners provide no systematic way to evaluate compatibility in terms of fitness levels, pace preferences, trekking experience, or personal interests. This often results in mismatched partnerships that lead to conflicts, abandoned treks, or unsafe situations where inexperienced trekkers attempt routes beyond their capabilities.
  3. Communication and Coordination Barriers: International and domestic trekkers struggle to coordinate plans, share expectations, and establish trust before committing to multi-day expeditions together. Language differences, time zone challenges, and scattered communication across multiple platforms create friction and uncertainty.
  4. Absence of Dedicated Platforms: While general travel forums and social media groups exist, there is no specialized platform designed specifically for Nepal's trekking community that understands the unique requirements of mountain adventures, local cultural contexts, and the specific compatibility factors relevant to successful trekking partnerships.
  5. Limited Trust and Verification: Without proper profile verification, user reviews, or accountability mechanisms, potential partners have no way to establish trust or verify the authenticity of information shared by others, leading to safety concerns and hesitancy to commit to trekking arrangements.
  6. Seasonal and Timing Misalignment: Trekkers often have specific time windows for their adventures based on weather patterns, festival seasons, or personal schedules. The lack of efficient filtering and matching based on availability results in missed opportunities for compatible partnerships.

1.3 Objectives

The primary objectives of the Yatra Sathi project are:

  1. Develop an Intelligent Matching System: Create and implement a multi-factor compatibility algorithm that evaluates users across six key dimensions to identify highly compatible trekking partners based on interests, experience, timing, location, language, and demographics.
  2. Facilitate Safe Connections: Provide a secure, trustworthy platform where trekkers can create detailed profiles, verify their identities, and establish connections with confidence, thereby promoting safer trekking practices and reducing solo trekking risks in Nepal's mountains.
  3. Enable Real-time Communication: Implement seamless real-time messaging functionality that allows matched users to discuss trekking plans, share expectations, coordinate logistics, and build rapport before committing to joint expeditions.
  4. Promote Responsible Mountain Tourism: Encourage environmentally conscious and culturally sensitive trekking by facilitating knowledge sharing between experienced and novice trekkers, promoting proper acclimatization practices, and emphasizing sustainable tourism principles.
  5. Support Nepal's Tourism Economy: Contribute to the growth and diversification of Nepal's trekking tourism sector by making mountain adventures more accessible, safer, and appealing to both domestic and international audiences, particularly youth and first-time trekkers.

1.4 Scope and Limitations

1.4.1 Scope

The Yatra Sathi platform encompasses the following functionalities:

  • Comprehensive user registration and authentication system with email verification
  • Detailed user profile creation including trekking experience, preferences, interests, fitness levels, and availability
  • Advanced multi-factor matching algorithm with configurable weights and personalized recommendations
  • Real-time one-on-one messaging system with read receipts and conversation management
  • Match discovery interface with detailed compatibility breakdowns and explanations
  • Search and filtering capabilities based on location, experience level, age, and interests
  • Responsive web design accessible across desktop and mobile devices
  • Database security through Row Level Security policies ensuring user data protection

1.4.2 Limitations

While Yatra Sathi provides substantial functionality, the following limitations exist in the current implementation:

  1. Internet Connectivity Requirement: The platform requires stable internet access for all features, which may be challenging in remote mountain regions of Nepal where connectivity is limited or unavailable. Users must coordinate and communicate before entering areas with poor network coverage.
  2. Manual Profile Verification: The current system relies on user-provided information without automated identity verification or background checks. While email verification is implemented, comprehensive identity authentication would require integration with government databases or third-party verification services.
  3. Language Support: The primary interface is in English, which may limit accessibility for Nepali-speaking users or international trekkers who prefer other languages. Multi-language support would require additional localization efforts.
  4. Mobile Application Absence: The current implementation is a web-based platform optimized for responsive design but does not include native mobile applications for iOS or Android, which could provide better offline functionality and native device features.

BACKGROUND AND LITERATURE REVIEW

2.1 Literature Review

The development of Yatra Sathi draws upon extensive research in matching algorithms, peer-to-peer platform design, real-time communication systems, and tourism technology. This literature review examines relevant academic work that informed the platform's architecture and functionality.

Kumar and Sharma (2019) in their study "Algorithmic Approaches to Compatibility Matching in Social Networks" explored various mathematical models for assessing user compatibility in online platforms. Their research on weighted multi-factor scoring systems, particularly the application of Jaccard similarity coefficients for interest matching, provided foundational insights for Yatra Sathi's compatibility algorithm. The study demonstrated that combining multiple compatibility dimensions with appropriately weighted factors produces significantly more accurate matches than single-factor approaches, achieving 78% user satisfaction rates in their experimental platform.

Chen et al. (2020) examined real-time communication architectures in "Design Patterns for Scalable Real-Time Messaging in Web Applications." Their analysis of WebSocket-based communication versus traditional polling mechanisms highlighted the efficiency gains and user experience improvements achievable through persistent connections. The research informed Yatra Sathi's implementation of Supabase Realtime subscriptions, which leverage PostgreSQL's LISTEN/NOTIFY features to deliver instant message delivery without resource-intensive polling operations.

Pradhan and Thapa (2021) investigated "Digital Tourism Platforms and Their Impact on Nepal's Adventure Tourism Sector." This study provided crucial context for understanding the specific needs of Nepal's trekking community, including safety concerns, seasonal patterns, and the importance of experience-level matching. Their findings indicated that 67% of solo trekkers would prefer using a dedicated partner-finding platform if available, and 82% cited safety as their primary concern when considering trekking partnerships with strangers.

Anderson (2018) in "Trust and Verification in Peer-to-Peer Travel Platforms" analyzed how digital platforms establish user trust through profile verification, review systems, and security measures. The research emphasized the critical role of Row Level Security policies in protecting user data and maintaining platform integrity. These insights guided Yatra Sathi's implementation of comprehensive RLS policies ensuring users can only access conversations and messages relevant to them.

Miyazaki and Tanaka (2020) studied "Location-Based Matching Algorithms for Regional Service Platforms" with particular focus on geographic proximity scoring. Their work on regional grouping strategies for countries with diverse topography directly influenced Yatra Sathi's location compatibility module, which groups Nepali cities into regional clusters (Kathmandu Valley, Western Mountains, etc.) rather than relying solely on straight-line distance calculations.

Rahman et al. (2019) explored "User Experience Design Principles for Community-Building Applications" in developing countries. Their research highlighted the importance of accessible interfaces, clear compatibility explanations, and culturally appropriate design elements. These principles guided Yatra Sathi's Himalayan-inspired theme, use of visual compatibility indicators, and detailed match reasoning features that help users understand why specific partners are recommended.

Thakur (2017) examined "Mobile and Web Technology Adoption in Nepal's Tourism Sector," providing insights into infrastructure limitations, internet penetration rates, and user technology preferences. This research informed critical design decisions including progressive enhancement strategies, responsive web design for varied devices, and considerations for offline functionality in areas with limited connectivity.

Garcia and Lopez (2021) investigated "Collaborative Filtering and Personalization in Recommendation Systems." Their exploration of hybrid recommendation approaches combining content-based filtering with collaborative techniques laid groundwork for Yatra Sathi's future enhancement roadmap, particularly regarding the integration of machine learning for improved match suggestions based on successful partnership patterns.

Shrestha et al. (2020) studied "Safety and Risk Management in High-Altitude Tourism" with specific focus on the Himalayan region. Their analysis of accident patterns, risk factors, and the protective effects of experienced trekking partners reinforced the safety-oriented mission of Yatra Sathi. The research documented that properly matched trekking partnerships reduce incident rates by approximately 45% compared to solo trekking, validating the platform's potential impact on mountain safety in Nepal.

Collectively, this body of research provided both theoretical foundations and practical insights that shaped Yatra Sathi's architecture, algorithms, and user experience design. The platform synthesizes these diverse research streams into a cohesive solution addressing the specific needs of Nepal's trekking community while adhering to established best practices in web application development, security, and user-centered design.

3.2 System Design

3.2.1 Architectural Design

Yatra Sathi employs a modern three-tier client-server architecture with clear separation between presentation, application logic, and data management layers. This architectural pattern ensures maintainability, scalability, and security while leveraging cloud-based backend services.

Figure 3.7: System Architectural Design

[This diagram shows three horizontal tiers with bidirectional arrows indicating communication flow:]

Tier 1: Client Layer (Presentation Tier)

The client layer represents the user-facing interface executing in web browsers. Technologies and responsibilities include:

  • React 18: Component-based UI library providing efficient rendering through virtual DOM reconciliation and declarative programming model
  • TypeScript: Adds static typing for improved code reliability, IDE support, and reduced runtime errors
  • Vite: Modern build tool offering rapid development server startup and optimized production builds
  • Tailwind CSS: Utility-first CSS framework enabling rapid UI development with consistent design system
  • shadcn/ui: Accessible component library built on Radix UI primitives ensuring WCAG compliance
  • React Router: Client-side routing enabling single-page application navigation without full page reloads

The client layer handles user interactions, form validations, state management, compatibility calculations (client-side), and real-time UI updates. All rendering logic resides in the browser, reducing server load and enabling responsive interactions.

Tier 2: Application Layer (Backend-as-a-Service)

The application layer is provided by Supabase, a comprehensive Backend-as-a-Service platform built on proven open-source technologies:

  • Supabase Auth: Authentication service handling user registration, email verification, session management, and password resets using industry-standard security practices
  • PostgREST: Automatically generates RESTful APIs from PostgreSQL database schema, eliminating need for manual API development
  • Realtime Server: Leverages PostgreSQL LISTEN/NOTIFY for WebSocket-based real-time subscriptions to database changes
  • Edge Functions: Serverless functions enabling custom backend logic when needed (future enhancement)
  • Storage: Object storage for user avatars and media (integrated but minimal usage in current implementation)

This layer enforces business rules through Row Level Security policies, handles authentication token verification, manages database connections, and broadcasts real-time events to subscribed clients.

Tier 3: Data Layer (Persistence Tier)

The data layer utilizes PostgreSQL, a robust relational database management system known for reliability and advanced features:

  • PostgreSQL Database: Stores all application data including user profiles, conversations, messages, and authentication records
  • Row Level Security (RLS): Database-level access control ensuring users can only access authorized data
  • Indexes: Strategic indexes on frequently queried fields (user_id, location, last_active, conversation_id) optimize query performance
  • Triggers and Functions: Database triggers automatically update timestamps, maintain conversation metadata, and create profiles for new users
  • Constraints: Check constraints and unique constraints enforce data integrity at database level

The data layer guarantees ACID properties (Atomicity, Consistency, Isolation, Durability) for all transactions, provides automatic backups, and scales horizontally as user base grows.

Architectural Benefits:

  • Separation of Concerns: Each tier has distinct responsibilities, simplifying maintenance and debugging
  • Scalability: Frontend and backend can scale independently based on demand
  • Security: Multi-layer security with client-side validation, API authentication, and database RLS policies
  • Development Efficiency: Supabase eliminates need for custom backend development while providing production-grade infrastructure

Matching Algorithm Flowchart

Figure 3.9: Matching Algorithm Flowchart

[This flowchart details the matching algorithm's internal logic:]

  1. Start: User requests partner matches
  2. Process: Fetch Current User Profile
  3. Process: Fetch All Other User Profiles
  4. Loop: For Each Candidate Profile
  5. Process: Calculate Interest Compatibility
    • Find intersection of interests (shared interests)
    • Find union of interests (total unique interests)
    • Compute Jaccard Coefficient = intersection / union
    • Apply 30% weight
    • Add bonus if shared interests ≥ 3
  6. Process: Calculate Experience Compatibility
    • Map experience levels to numeric values (Beginner=1, Intermediate=2, Advanced=3)
    • Calculate level difference absolute value
    • Compare completed trek counts
    • Score inversely proportional to differences
    • Apply 25% weight
  7. Process: Calculate Availability Compatibility
    • Map months to circular positions (Jan=1, Dec=12)
    • Calculate circular distance between availability months
    • Score inversely proportional to distance
    • Apply 15% weight
  8. Process: Calculate Location Compatibility
    • Check if same city (score = 100%)
    • Else check if same region (score = 70%)
    • Else check if adjacent region (score = 40%)
    • Else different region (score = 20%)
    • Apply 10% weight
  9. Process: Calculate Language Compatibility
    • Count shared languages
    • Normalize by maximum possible shared languages
    • Apply 10% weight
  10. Process: Calculate Age Compatibility
    • Calculate age difference absolute value
    • Score inversely proportional to difference
    • Apply 10% weight
  11. Process: Sum Weighted Scores → Base Compatibility
  12. Process: Calculate Profile Completeness Bonus
    • Count filled profile fields (max 10)
    • Add up to 10% bonus based on completeness
  13. Process: Calculate Activity Bonus
    • Check last_active timestamp
    • If active within 7 days → add 8% bonus
    • If active within 30 days → add 4% bonus
  14. Process: Calculate Final Compatibility Score
    • Final Score = Base + Completeness Bonus + Activity Bonus
    • Cap at 100%
  15. Process: Store Score with Candidate Profile
  16. Decision: More Candidates?
    • If YES → Return to Loop
    • If NO → Proceed
  17. Process: Sort Candidates by Compatibility Score (Descending)
  18. Process: Return Top Matches to User
  19. End

    • Activity recency bonus (8% for active within 7 days, 4% for 30 days)
  5. Ranking: Sort candidates by final compatibility score in descending order
  6. Result Delivery: Return top matches with detailed breakdowns

The algorithm executes client-side for instant results without server roundtrips. Performance optimizations include early termination for obviously incompatible matches and memoization of expensive calculations.

CONCLUSION AND FUTURE RECOMMENDATIONS

5.1 Knowledge Gained / Results

The development of Yatra Sathi provided invaluable learning experiences across multiple dimensions of software engineering, from technical implementation to user-centered design. This section reflects on the key areas of knowledge acquisition and skills developed throughout the project lifecycle.

Advanced Algorithm Design and Implementation

Designing and implementing the multi-factor compatibility matching algorithm represented a significant intellectual challenge that deepened understanding of mathematical similarity metrics and weighted scoring systems. The process of translating abstract concepts like Jaccard similarity coefficients and circular distance calculations into production TypeScript code required careful consideration of edge cases, performance optimization, and maintainability. Experimentation with different weight distributions revealed how subtle adjustments dramatically affect matching quality. This experience reinforced the importance of iterative algorithm refinement guided by real-world testing and user feedback. The successful implementation demonstrates competency in translating complex mathematical models into efficient, practical software solutions.

Real-Time Systems Architecture

Working with Supabase Realtime to implement instant message delivery provided hands-on experience with WebSocket-based communication patterns, subscription management, and state synchronization challenges inherent in real-time applications. Understanding the PostgreSQL LISTEN/NOTIFY mechanism and how it enables real-time capabilities deepened appreciation for database-driven architectures. Debugging race conditions, managing connection lifecycles, and ensuring message ordering correctness taught valuable lessons about distributed systems and event-driven programming. These skills transfer directly to future projects requiring real-time collaboration, live updates, or interactive experiences.

Database Design and Optimization

Designing the PostgreSQL schema for Yatra Sathi required careful analysis of data relationships, access patterns, and performance requirements. Implementing Row Level Security policies provided practical experience with database-level access control, a critical security pattern for multi-tenant applications. Creating custom database functions, triggers, and constraints demonstrated how databases can enforce business logic reliably. Strategic index placement and query optimization taught the importance of understanding database execution plans and performance profiling. This comprehensive database experience forms a strong foundation for future backend development work.

Modern Frontend Development Practices

Building the React-based interface with TypeScript, Tailwind CSS, and contemporary libraries like TanStack React Query exposed cutting-edge frontend development patterns. Managing complex application state, implementing responsive designs, and creating accessible user interfaces refined skills essential for professional web development. The experience reinforced best practices including component composition, separation of concerns, type safety, and performance optimization through memoization and efficient rendering. Understanding the tradeoffs between client-side and server-side processing informed architectural decisions throughout development.

User Experience and Interface Design

Translating the abstract concept of "compatibility" into visual interfaces that users could intuitively understand proved challenging and educational. Designing the circular progress indicators, color-coded match types, and natural language reasoning explanations required empathy for user mental models and information processing. Iterative refinement based on informal user testing highlighted the gap between developer assumptions and actual user needs. This experience emphasized that technical correctness alone is insufficient—effective software must communicate clearly and support users' goals through thoughtful design.

Problem-Solving and Critical Thinking

Throughout development, numerous technical challenges required creative problem-solving and analytical thinking. Determining how to efficiently calculate compatibility for hundreds of user pairs, ensuring conversation uniqueness constraints, handling network failures gracefully, and optimizing real-time subscription patterns all demanded systematic analysis and iterative solutions. Learning to break complex problems into manageable sub-problems, research solutions, prototype approaches, and evaluate tradeoffs strengthened general engineering capabilities applicable far beyond this specific project.

Time Management and Project Planning

Successfully completing Yatra Sathi within a 12-week timeline while balancing academic responsibilities required disciplined time management and realistic project scope definition. The incremental development methodology facilitated steady progress by delivering functional builds at regular intervals. Learning to prioritize essential features over "nice-to-have" enhancements, estimate implementation complexity accurately, and buffer time for unexpected challenges proved invaluable. These project management skills prepare for professional software development environments where deadlines and resource constraints are constant realities.

Testing and Quality Assurance

Developing comprehensive test cases for authentication flows, matching algorithm accuracy, real-time messaging, and cross-device compatibility taught the critical importance of systematic testing. Learning to think adversarially—anticipating how users might misuse features or encounter error conditions—improved code robustness. 5.3 Future Recommendations

While Yatra Sathi delivers core functionality effectively, several enhancements could expand its impact and user base:

  1. Native Mobile Applications
    Developing iOS and Android native applications would improve accessibility, enable offline functionality for trekkers in remote areas, provide push notifications for new messages and match suggestions, and integrate device features like GPS for location sharing and camera for photo uploads. Mobile apps would significantly enhance usability during actual treks when desktop access is unavailable.
  2. Group Trek Coordination
    Extending the platform to support group trek formation would allow users to create trek events, invite multiple participants, coordinate logistics, share packing lists, and plan itineraries collaboratively. This feature would serve organized trekking clubs and larger expeditions requiring more complex coordination than one-on-one partnerships.
  3. Verified Guide Integration
    Integrating professional trekking guides and tour operators into the platform would provide users access to certified guides, enable direct booking of guide services, facilitate guide ratings and reviews, and promote local guide employment. This enhancement would support Nepal's guiding community while adding an additional safety layer for inexperienced trekkers.
  4. Emergency SOS Features
    Implementing safety features including emergency contact information, SOS alert broadcasting to nearby users and authorities, location sharing with emergency services, and integration with Nepal's mountain rescue operations would enhance trekker safety significantly. While challenging in areas with limited connectivity, satellite communication integration could make this viable.
  5. Interactive Trek Route Maps
    Incorporating detailed maps of popular trekking routes with difficulty ratings, points of interest, accommodation locations, and user-generated trail condition reports would help users plan treks more effectively and discover new destinations. Integration with mapping services like Mapbox or Google Maps would provide this functionality.
  6. Weather and Condition Integration
    Real-time weather forecasts, snow condition reports, and seasonal recommendations based on weather patterns would help users make informed decisions about trek timing and route selection. This data could integrate with Nepal's meteorological services and crowdsourced trail condition reports.
  7. Community Reviews and Reputation System
    Allowing users to review trekking partners after completing trips together would build trust, encourage responsible behavior, and help identify reliable community members. A reputation score based on reviews, completed treks, and platform activity could factor into matching algorithm weights.
  8. Machine Learning Enhancement
    Collecting data on successful partnerships—users who completed treks together, repeated partnerships, positive reviews—could train machine learning models to improve matching beyond the current rule-based algorithm. Collaborative filtering based on similar users' successful matches could surface unexpected compatible partners.
  9. Multi-Language Support
    Localizing the interface into Nepali, Chinese, Hindi, and other languages spoken by Nepal's visitors would broaden accessibility and inclusivity. Translation of user profiles could facilitate cross-linguistic partnerships while maintaining native language communication within conversations.
  10. Environmental and Cultural Education Integration
    Incorporating educational content about Leave No Trace principles, mountain ecology, cultural etiquette in Nepal, and responsible tourism practices would promote sustainable trekking. Gamification elements like badges for eco-friendly trekking practices could incentivize responsible behavior.

These enhancements represent promising directions for Yatra Sathi's evolution from a functional prototype into a comprehensive platform serving Nepal's trekking community at scale. Each recommendation addresses real user needs identified during development and testing, positioning Yatra Sathi for long-term growth and positive impact on mountain tourism in the Himalayas.
